# Three Roles for Civic Witness

Field protocol accompanying the Civic Witness Charter. This is not legal advice.

## Three Roles for Civic Witness

Three people can share these roles. With fewer people, adapt to the situation and available capacity. Safety and urgent care take priority over completing a record.

### One: The Witness

Observe and record from a safe position without obstructing others or interfering with emergency assistance. There is no need to move closer to complete the record.

Record what happens as plainly as possible. Avoid commentary that obscures speech or substitutes interpretation for observation. Where safe, capture context as well as the immediate action.

### Two: The Archivist / Journalist

Keep a detailed account and help preserve the original material. When safe and possible, record:

- **When and where:** date, time, time zone, and precise location. Mark estimates as estimates and note any known discrepancy in the recording device’s clock.
- **Who:** names if available, badge or identification numbers, agencies, ranks, vehicle markings, and who gave or carried out orders. Note how each identity was established. Do not guess; record obscured or unavailable identification as such.
- **What happened:** the sequence before, during, and afterward; who did what; words spoken, orders or threats, force used, visible injuries, and requests for help or medical care. Use quotation marks only for words remembered accurately; identify paraphrases.
- **Position and limits:** where you were, what you could see or hear, and anything obstructing your view. Separate direct observation, information reported by others, and inference. Record uncertainty and relevant context, including details that complicate an initial impression.
- **Supporting evidence:** original recordings, photographs, documents, nearby cameras, and witnesses willing to be contacted. Keep contact details secure and out of public versions.

Write notes as soon as practical. Record both when the event occurred and when the notes were made. Preserve the first account; date later additions and corrections. Let witnesses give their own accounts without coaching or harmonizing their recollections.

### Three: The Guardian

Watch safety, distance, exits, and the needs of affected people. Invite others to witness or document only when safe and voluntary.

Offer grounding support without requiring calmness or participation. Say that help is on the way only if that is known. Where safe and appropriate, help someone request medical assistance or contact a trusted person.

Ask permission before gathering names or contact information where possible. Explain the intended use, collect only what is needed, and keep it private. Do not pressure someone to identify themselves, give an account, or appear in public material.

### Audio integrity

Keep speech, names, commands, and contextual sounds intelligible where possible. If loud alerts are needed, place them away from the immediate recording area when practical. Never delay an urgent warning to protect a recording.

## Preserve the record

Keep original notes and unedited recordings, including their metadata and original filenames. Keep secure backups with restricted access. Social-media uploads or messaging copies may lose quality or metadata; do not rely on them as the only copy.

Make transcripts, excerpts, enhancements, or redactions as separate copies. Label them clearly and record what changed, by whom, and when. Preserve the unaltered originals securely.

Keep a dated handling log: who created or obtained each item, when and where it originated, who received or accessed it, and any copying or changes made. This history of handling is often called the chain of custody. File hashes can provide an additional integrity check when appropriate support is available.

## Share with care

Preserving evidence does not require publishing it. Public sharing and confidential evidence preservation require separate decisions.

Protect affected people and witnesses. Seek consent before sharing identifying information where possible; assess whether publication could expose someone to retaliation or other harm. Use redacted copies for public sharing while keeping originals secure.

Provide material through a secure, appropriate route to trusted legal counsel or a suitable independent investigative body. Qualified counsel can help identify what a particular case needs. Preserve relevant material while legal proceedings or preservation obligations are pending; seek advice before deleting it.

Details that seem small today may make accountability possible tomorrow.

## The Accordion Principle

The roles can expand or contract as needed. One person may hold all three, but does not have to perform every task. Take care of yourself and others; step back when needed. Others may join voluntarily as capacity allows.

One Guardian. One Witness. One Journalist or Archivist. Shared roles, held with care.
